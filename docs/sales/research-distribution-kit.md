## 2. LinkedIn article (Cam's personal profile, long-form)

**Title:** The hygiene chair is still empty. Here's what the 2026 data says.

**Subtitle / hook:** I went looking for real numbers on the dental hiring shortage and threw out half of what I found. Here's what held up.

---

I spent ten years on the business side of dentistry. If there's one sentence I heard more than any other in that time, it's "we can't find a hygienist."

Everyone in the industry agrees the shortage is real. What I couldn't find was a single place that showed how bad it is, with sources you could check. A lot of the numbers floating around dental hiring content are unsourced, outdated, or misread. So at DSO Hire I built a report from the primary data: the ADA Health Policy Institute's quarterly survey of dentists, the Bureau of Labor Statistics, and HRSA's federal workforce projections. Here's what it shows.

**Nine in ten, every quarter**

In the ADA Health Policy Institute's Q3 2026 survey, 88.3 percent of dentists who had recruited a hygienist in the prior three months called it "very" or "extremely challenging." That share has stayed between 87.7 and 91.7 percent every quarter since the start of 2024.

That steadiness is what stands out to me. This isn't a post-COVID spike that's fading. It has been flat for almost three years.

**Fewer than half actually fill the role**

The Q3 2026 survey also asked whether dentists filled the role. Only 42.1 percent of those recruiting a hygienist did, almost the same as a year earlier. For dental assistants it was 66.8 percent.

Even the hires that happen take a while. Among filled hygienist roles, 30.8 percent had been open one to three months, 19.2 percent three to six months, and 21.2 percent more than six months, per the same HPI survey. One in five hygiene chairs sat empty for half a year before someone filled it.

**Four in ten don't have enough hygienists**

In Q1 2026, HPI asked dentists whether their staffing was adequate. Only 60.3 percent said yes for hygienists, against 73.5 percent for assistants and 79.3 percent for administrative staff.

Asked why hygienist recruiting is so hard, 66.5 percent pointed to not enough applicants and 36.8 percent to demands for high wages or benefits. Pay is part of this. On current BLS series, production and nonsupervisory pay in offices of dentists rose 24.6 percent from January 2021 to August 2026, while consumer prices rose 27.2 percent (our calculation, with the series named in the report). HPI's own reading is 21 percent against 27. The two figures differ, but both show staff pay trailing inflation.

**DSOs feel it most**

This is the part that matters most for multi-location groups. When HPI asked dentists in late 2025 to name their top challenges for 2026, staffing ranked first among DSO-affiliated dentists, at 59.3 percent, against 53.8 percent for dentists outside DSOs. (HPI doesn't publish the DSO subgroup size, so read that as directional.)

At the same time, DSOs are where new dentists are going. HPI reports that 16.1 percent of U.S. dentists were DSO-affiliated in 2024, up from 7.2 percent in 2015, and 26.5 percent of dentists up to 10 years out of school. In ADEA's survey of 2025 dental school seniors, 32 percent of those heading into private practice said they intend to join a DSO.

That's good news for associate recruiting. But every new associate needs a hygiene schedule and assistants to run it, and that's where the bottleneck is.

**The next decade doesn't look easier**

The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects about 15,000 hygienist openings and 53,000 dental assistant openings a year through 2035. HRSA projects a shortage of 33,220 full-time-equivalent hygienists in 2038, larger than its projected shortage of general dentists.

**What multi-location groups can do**

None of this gets solved by one tool or one tactic. But a few things follow directly from the data:

- **Plan hygienist hires in months, not weeks.** Half of filled hygienist roles took one to six months. Start searches before a departure or a new location opens, and budget for temp coverage in between.
- **Expect to lose some searches.** With fewer than half of searches ending in a hire, keep a pipeline open for recurring roles instead of starting from zero every time.
- **Check your offers against the market.** The BLS May 2025 medians are $98,100 for hygienists and $48,070 for assistants. Candidates name pay and benefits, and HPI found practices with adequate staffing offer benefits more often.
- **Treat retention as recruiting.** DANB's 2024 survey found about a quarter of assistants left their job in the prior year. Keeping the people you have is the cheapest hire you'll make.
- **Make the support-staff side keep pace with associate hiring.** Your DSO's pull with new dentists only helps if the hygiene schedule can absorb them.

**Where to find the full picture**

The full report has every source, the charts, and the caveats: who's in the HPI panel (it skews toward solo and small-group owners), where sources disagree, and a list of widely shared statistics we couldn't verify and left out.

A disclosure: I sell hiring software to dental groups, so weigh my framing the way you would anyone's. The numbers come from the ADA, BLS and HRSA, not from us.

Full report, sources and charts: https://dsohire.com/research/dental-hiring-shortage-2026

I'd like to hear from people running hiring across multiple locations. Which of these numbers matches what you're seeing, and which doesn't?

**Recommendation: lowest-cost first step for a pre-revenue vendor**

Don't pay $8,000 for Standard membership yet. The free first step is one short email to **membership@theadso.org**. Ask whether 2026 Industry Partner dues have changed, what the new Leadership tier is, whether there's a startup or first-year rate, and whether ADSO's Workforce Task Force or *The Brush Up* would accept the hiring shortage report as a member resource. Then plan to attend the **June 2027 Summit** as a non-member attendee (allowed in 2026, price not published) and revisit membership once DSO Hire has paying customers. Next Level (Oct 21–23) requires membership for vendors, so skip it this year.
