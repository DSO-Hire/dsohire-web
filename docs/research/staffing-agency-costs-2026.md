# Fact dossier: What dental staffing agencies actually charge (2026)

Prepared for: DSO Hire research report (dsohire.com). Audience: operators at multi-location dental groups.
Compiled: 2026-10-19. All sources accessed 2026-10-19 unless a publication date is given.

**Method and caveats**

- Every figure below was seen on a page fetched on 2026-10-19 (or pulled from the official BLS Public Data API). Figures that surfaced only in search snippets, third-party aggregators, or pages that refused the fetch are listed under "Rejected / unverifiable", not in the body.
- Quotes were captured with an automated page reader. Before publishing, spot-check each quote against the live page (pricing pages change often, and several vendors update monthly).
- Source types: **government**, **association**, **academic**, **vendor-self-reported** (a company describing its own prices), **agency marketing** (a company describing competitors or the market), **press**.
- Very few traditional dental staffing agencies publish their fees. Most hard numbers in this dossier come from (a) one general staffing firm with a dental division that publishes a rate card, (b) app-based temp marketplaces that publish their own fees, and (c) government wage data. Treat "industry typical" ranges as directional.
- Calculations marked **[derived]** are DSO Hire arithmetic on cited inputs, not figures from any source.

## Key findings

1. **Permanent placement is usually priced as a share of first-year salary.** The one agency with a dental division that publishes a rate card charges 20 to 30% of first-year base salary for direct hire [7]; an uncited vendor guide puts the range at 15 to 25% [14]. On BLS May 2025 medians, 20 to 30% works out to about $19,600 to $29,400 for a hygienist and $9,600 to $14,400 for a dental assistant [1][2][7] [derived].
2. **Dentist-only recruiters sometimes charge flat fees.** Caris Partners publishes $5,000 to $15,000 per dentist or specialist hire [10]; others advertise "flat rate" or "below industry average" pricing without a number [11][12].
3. **The standard guarantee is 90 days.** Henry Schein Dental Recruitment Services and The Doc Hunters both publish a 90-day guarantee [11][12], and Frontline calls 90 days the "industry standard" while selling a 5-year warranty (pro-rated credit, not a cash refund) [7][9].
4. **Temp marketplaces publish fees of 12% to 25% of the clinician's pay, or flat per-shift fees.** GoTu charges 22.5% of a hygienist's shift rate and $60 to $300 flat for other roles [15]; Teero charges 12% plus W-2 payroll costs [16]; onDiem gives ADA members a flat 25% [18]; TempStars charges $38 USD per completed shift [20]. Traditional temp agencies bundle their markup into an hourly bill rate; the one published rate card shows a 50 to 85% markup [7].
5. **"Free" permanent hires are now common on temp platforms.** Teero, TempStars, onDiem (temp-to-perm), Cloud Dentistry (self-service), DirectDental, and Princess Dental Staffing all state $0 placement or buyout fees on their own pages [16][20][19][24][26][27]; most charge a subscription or per-shift fee instead.
6. **Job boards cost hundreds, not thousands, per month.** DentalPost's job-posting subscription is $259/month [33]; LinkedIn's own example is a $10/day budget for 30 days, "$300 or less" [36]; Indeed prices sponsored jobs by auction, mostly pay-per-started-application, with no fixed public price [34][35].
7. **Vacancies are long and costly.** In Q3 2026, one in five dentists said hygienist positions were open more than six months before being filled [6]. DANB/DALE Foundation research estimates an unfilled assistant role puts more than $21,000 in revenue at risk, and nearly $110,000 if it stays open a year [39].
8. **Generic "turnover cost" multiples are wide and old.** Gallup (2019) puts replacement at one-half to two times annual salary [37]; SHRM (2022) puts average cost per hire near $4,700 [38]. The widely repeated "a bad hire costs 30% of first-year earnings (US Dept. of Labor)" could not be traced to any DOL source (see Rejected).

## Conflicts and ranges to present honestly

- **Perm fee %:** 15 to 25% (uncited vendor guide [14]) vs 20 to 30% (published rate card [7]). Present as "roughly 15 to 30% of first-year salary, with the only published dental rate card at 20 to 30%."
- **Dentist searches:** flat $5,000 to $15,000 [10] vs percentage models that would imply $34,000 to $51,000 on a median general-dentist salary [derived]. Present both models.
- **Guarantee remedy:** "free replacement" [11] vs "pro-rated credit" [7] vs "replace at no additional search fee" [9] (the last two are the same firm, different pages).
- **TempStars fee:** pricing page shows "$73" without currency [21]; FAQ clarifies $73 CAD and $38 USD [20]. Use $38 for US.
- **Temp markup:** published platform fees 12 to 25% [15][16][18] vs one agency rate card at 50 to 85% [7] vs competitor estimates of 30 to 60% [14] or $15 to $60/hr [22]. The platform numbers exclude employer payroll taxes on W-2 models.
- **Dentist pay baseline:** BLS general-dentist median $170,950 and mean $191,350 [4]; BLS all-dentists median $176,110 [3]; ADA HPI owner net income $215,320 [5]. Different populations.
- **Kwikly 15%:** only verified on a competitor's comparison page [17], not on Kwikly's own pages.
